Field workflows

Planning departments use QField across the planning cycle:

  • Site surveys and pre-application assessments
  • Zoning compliance checks against the overlay that governs the parcel
  • Building permit inspections and development monitoring
  • Enforcement documentation with dated, geolocated photo evidence
  • Land use surveys and plan monitoring over time
  • Public consultation notes captured against specific locations

The plan, on the parcel, in your hand

Planning decisions are made in the office and tested on the ground, and the gap between the two is where enforcement cases are lost. QField closes it by putting the zoning overlay, the cadastral parcel, the permit record, and the aerial imagery on the tablet, available offline. An inspector standing on a site can see what is permitted there before deciding whether what is built matches it.

The evidence follows the same logic. A photograph taken in QField carries its coordinate, its timestamp, and the inspector who took it, attached to the feature it documents. Assembling an enforcement pack stops being an archaeology exercise across a camera roll, an email thread, and a notebook.

One workflow with QGIS and QFieldCloud

Prepare zoning layers, parcels, and inspection forms in QGIS . Push the project to every device through QFieldCloud . Planners and inspectors work offline and sync back into the same project the office plans from, with conflict-safe merges.

Common Challenges

  • Planners arriving on site without the zoning overlay that governs the parcel
  • Enforcement photos that cannot be tied to a date, a location, or an inspector
  • Site notes captured on paper and typed up days later, if at all
  • Office and field working from different versions of the same plan
  • Limited connectivity on new developments at the edge of the network
  • Long enforcement timelines because the evidence pack takes weeks to assemble

  • Every observation carries its location, timestamp, and author
  • Open source, with no per-seat licensing for planning and enforcement staff

Frequently Asked

Can we load zoning maps, overlays, and aerial imagery for offline use?

Yes. Prepare the layers in QGIS and package them onto the device. Zoning boundaries, parcels, permits, and imagery are all available on site without a network connection.

How does QField help with zoning enforcement evidence?

Every photo is captured against the feature it documents, with location, timestamp, and the inspector's identity attached. That is the evidence pack, assembled as the inspection happens rather than afterwards.

Can inspectors see the permitted use for a parcel while standing on it?

Yes. Tap the parcel and QField shows its attributes from the project, including zoning class, permitted use, and any linked permit records.

Can we track a development against its approved plan over time?

Yes. Revisit the same site across a project's life and capture dated observations against the same feature, building a chronological record of progress or non-compliance.

How do we get field observations back into our reporting?

Data syncs to QFieldCloud and lands in the same QGIS project the office works from. Export to any format QGIS supports, or connect QGIS directly to PostGIS for live dashboards.

Can planners annotate and sketch on site?

Yes. QField supports freehand drawing, geometry editing, and free-text notes alongside structured form fields.

Does QField work in areas with no mobile coverage?

Yes. QField runs fully offline by default and syncs when the device is next online.

Can several inspectors cover a district at the same time?

Yes. With QFieldCloud each inspector works on their own offline copy, and changes merge automatically on sync.

About QField

QField is the leading professional fieldwork app used in enterprise settings for efficient geospatial data collection and management. As a Digital Public Good, QField not only excels in professional applications but also contributes significantly to advancing at least six of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) , promoting a more sustainable and equitable future.

Open-source

QField is released under the GNU Public License (GPL) version 2 or later. Developing QField under this license means that you can inspect and modify the source code and guarantees that you will always have access to a QGIS based field data collection app that is free of cost and can be freely modified.
