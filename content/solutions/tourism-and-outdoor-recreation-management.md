## Field workflows

Park and recreation teams use QField to look after the places people visit:

- Trail mapping with track logs, including new routes and reroutes
- Trail condition surveys, erosion, and hazard reporting
- Signage and waymarking inventories
- Visitor facility inspections, from toilets and shelters to car parks and viewpoints
- Campsite and hut inventories
- Volunteer maintenance and work party records

## Built for the backcountry

Most of a park is where the mobile network is not. QField is designed for exactly that. Everything the team needs, including basemaps, terrain, existing trails, and asset layers, is loaded onto the device before it leaves the office, so a ranger two days from a road has the same tools as one at the visitor centre.

The forms matter as much as the map. Trail condition ratings, sign types, and facility checks are set up once in QGIS, so a seasonal ranger in their first week records the same way as a twenty year veteran. Every photo stays attached to the asset it shows, and every visit adds to that asset's history.

## One workflow with QGIS and QFieldCloud

Prepare the trail and asset layers in [QGIS](https://qgis.org). Push the project to every ranger and volunteer through [QFieldCloud](https://qfield.cloud). Teams work offline across the park and sync when they come back into coverage, with conflict-safe merges when two teams have visited the same asset.
