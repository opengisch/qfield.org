## Field workflows

Civil protection teams use QField to understand risk before an emergency and to respond when one happens:

- Hazard mapping for floods, landslides, wildfires, and industrial risks
- Critical asset and infrastructure checks, from shelters and hospitals to bridges and pumping stations
- Vulnerability and exposure surveys of buildings and communities
- Evacuation route and assembly point inspections
- Emergency drills and exercises using live field workflows
- Post-event inspections and damage records

## Practise with the tool you will use on the day

The worst time to learn a new app is in the middle of an emergency. Civil protection work has the advantage of time to prepare, and QField makes the most of it. The same project that holds your hazard maps and asset register can carry your emergency forms, so risk assessments, drills, and real events all run through one tool that teams already know.

Germany's KulturGutRetter project, which builds rapid response capacity for cultural heritage in emergencies, took that approach. Its QField field kits sync over a local server and router, so they keep working offline, and they were put to the test in a full scale disaster drill in 2024.

## One workflow with QGIS and QFieldCloud

Maintain your hazard layers and asset registers in [QGIS](https://qgis.org). Push the project to every device through [QFieldCloud](https://qfield.cloud), or run your own server where the data has to stay inside your network. Every change is attributed to the person who made it, which is what an after-action review or an audit will ask for.
