## Field workflows

Heritage bodies, archaeologists, and volunteer groups use QField to document and protect the past:

- Heritage and monument inventories
- Condition surveys and repeat monitoring visits
- Excavation context and finds recording
- Archaeological field survey
- Heritage impact assessment for development projects
- Emergency documentation and rescue of heritage at risk

## From paper maps to one shared database

For fifteen years, volunteers monitoring the Westwall, the WWII fortification line along Germany's western border, worked with paper maps and spreadsheets, each on their own island. Today, their coordinator builds a project in QGIS, puts it on QFieldCloud, and volunteers pick it up and head into the field. Each visit adds a new observation point with photos and condition notes, and the coordinator sees the results in QGIS within minutes. The approach now covers around 20,000 structures along a 600 kilometre zone.

At the other end of the scale, archaeologists use QField on open area excavations to record contexts and finds directly into the project GIS. That removes the step of digitising field registers after the dig, and keeps the site record current while work is still underway.

## One workflow with QGIS and QFieldCloud

Prepare the heritage project in [QGIS](https://qgis.org), with historic maps, existing inventories, and recording forms. Share it through [QFieldCloud](https://qfield.cloud) with the staff and volunteers who need it. Everyone records the same way, and every record comes back to a single standardised database you can query and analyse.
